PHASE 01LITERATURE REVIEW

Down the
Rabbit Hole

Dozens of papers, and they disagree. Then I stopped reading conclusions and started reading methods.

The puzzle

Traditional game theory says no one should trust strangers with their money. The rational move is to keep it. Yet in Berg, Dickhaut and McCabe's (1995) Trust Game, people invest. Consistently. Across cultures.

Why? Two main explanations

Social preferences

Altruism, reciprocity, fairness norms. We trust because we care about others.

Risk attitudes

Different perceptions of uncertainty. Maybe trust is just how we handle social risk.

The confusion

The literature is full of contradictory findings

PREDICTION VS. NO PREDICTION
Some studies find risk preferences predict trust. Others find they don't.
Schechter (2007) vs. Houser et al. (2010)
EXCEEDS VS. DOES NOT EXCEED
Some find trust levels exceed risk taking. Others find risk taking exceeds trust.
Rățală et al. (2019) vs. Bohnet & Zeckhauser (2004)

Then I saw it

I started tracking what each study actually measured. Two methodological problems kept appearing.

1Different stakes and probabilities

Stakes and probability ranges varied wildly: small bets in one study, large ones in the next, extreme probabilities here, middling ones there.

Insight. Prospect Theory has different parameters for different contexts. Maybe they were testing different parts of the curve?

2Mismatched uncertainty

Studies were comparing apples to oranges. In risk, you know the dice is fair (known probabilities). In trust, you have no idea (ambiguity).

Insight. These are fundamentally different types of uncertainty. We need to control for this.

THE BREAKTHROUGH QUESTION

Can Prospect Theory explain trust, once you control for stakes, probabilities and uncertainty?

This became the thesis

The framework: Prospect Theory

Outcome sensitivity (α)
The difference between $0 and $10 feels bigger than between $100 and $110
Loss aversion (λ)
Losing $10 feels worse than gaining $10 feels good
Probability weighting (γ+, γ−)
We overweight small probabilities and underweight large ones. A 1% chance feels huge.

What needed to happen

  1. 1Matched probabilities. Trust and Risk games with identical outcome probabilities.
  2. 2Controlled uncertainty. Match the type of uncertainty: known probabilities for both.
  3. 3Optimal design. Use Fisher information to select highly informative trials.
  4. 4Model comparison. Compare candidate models to see what really differs.
