PHASE 03TRIAL-SET OPTIMISATION

Building the
Machine

How do you pick 200 trials out of 17,955 possible choices?

The problem

The design calls for a few hundred trials. But which ones?

Investments range from 1 to 10 tokens. Probabilities from 5% to 95%. Outcomes from 0 to 39 tokens. The full factorial space contains 17,955 possible trials.

The challenge

Not all trials are equally informative. Some tell us a lot about Prospect Theory parameters. Others are redundant or uninformative. We need a systematic method to select the best ones.

STEP 3

Certainty equivalents

For every single trial (17,955) and every single parameter combination (6.25M), calculate the certainty equivalent: how much is this gamble worth to a person with these specific risk preferences?

This required massive parallel computing. We computed 112 billion certainty equivalents to build a complete map of predicted behaviour.

STEP 5DERIVATIVES

Fisher information, one derivative at a time

Fisher information quantifies how much a trial tells you about a parameter. It is based on derivatives: how much does the certainty equivalent change when you tweak α, λ, γ+ or γ−?

STEP 6

D-optimal sequential selection

We don't just pick random trials. We use an algorithm that selects trials one by one.

  1. 1Pick the trial that maximises information about the parameters, given what we already know
  2. 2Add it to the set. Update the information matrix.
  3. 3Repeat, towards a target of 300. Keeping the set balanced stopped it at 286.

The 286 trials ran in the first pilot, each participant in one condition. For the second pilot, in both conditions, the set grew into a pool of 403. From that pool the same selection kept the final 200 trials, with at least five trials at each of the 19 probability levels. Every participant plays these 200 trials twice: once with a person, once with a lottery.
